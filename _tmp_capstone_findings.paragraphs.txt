17: ABSTRACT
18: The Automated Scholarship Records Management System was developed to make the scholarship process in the Local Government Unit of San Enrique faster, more organized, and easier to manage. Before the system was created, scholarship applications were handled mostly through manual recording, paper-based submissions, and repeated checking of documents. Because of this, staff had to spend more time sorting records, tracking applicant progress, and updating statuses, while students had limited ways to monitor their applications.
19: To solve these problems, the researchers created a web-based system that simplifies the scholarship process from application up to payout scheduling. Students can register, fill out their application online, upload requirements, submit their digital signature, track their status, and receive notifications about updates such as document review, interview schedules, Statement of Account (SOA) submission, and payout schedules. On the other hand, staff and administrators can verify documents, schedule interviews, process SOA submissions, manage payouts, post announcements, and generate reports more efficiently.
20: With the system in place, scholarship records can be handled in a more accurate, secure, and organized way. It helps lessen manual work, avoids repetitive tasks, improves transparency, and provides a more convenient experience for both applicants and LGU personnel. Overall, the system serves as a practical solution for improving scholarship management in the municipality.

23: Introduction
24: Around the world, scholarship programs play an important role in helping students gain access to education, especially those who face financial difficulties. As scholarship programs continue to expand, many governments and educational institutions have turned to web-based and automated systems to manage applications, records, and communication more effectively. Research shows that automating these processes helps reduce manual workload, minimize errors, improve transparency, and ensure that information is delivered to applicants in a timely and organized manner (Al-Ayyubi & Maulana, 2023).
25: In the Philippines, Local Government Units (LGUs) actively support education by offering locally funded scholarship programs. The Municipality of San Enrique, Negros Occidental reflects this commitment by providing financial assistance to qualified students. However, the current scholarship process still depends largely on manual practices such as posting announcements on social media, using handwritten application forms, maintaining records in spreadsheets, and requiring physical submission of documents. Studies indicate that these manual methods often lead to delays, miscommunication, repeated submissions, and difficulties in monitoring and retrieving scholarship records (Prijayanti et al., 2020).
26: To respond to these challenges, this study proposes the development of a web-based Automated Scholarship Records Management System for the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental. The proposed system is designed to centralize scholarship records, streamline application processing, track application status, and improve communication through SMS notifications. While physical document verification remains necessary for accounting, auditing, and approval purposes, the system aims to improve efficiency, transparency, and accessibility, resulting in a more organized and reliable scholarship management process for both LGU staff and scholarship applicants.

68: Scope and Limitations
69: This study focuses on the design, development, and implementation of a web-based Automated Scholarship Records Management System for the Local Government Unit (LGU) of San Enrique, Negros Occidental. The system is intended to support and improve the existing scholarship management process by providing a centralized platform for applicant registration, online scholarship application, document upload, application status tracking, applicant and scholar record management, interview scheduling, and notification delivery through SMS with One-Time Password (OTP) mobile number verification. It also includes features for monitoring application progress, managing scholarship-related announcements, organizing submitted records, and recording scholarship disbursement details for documentation and tracking purposes.
70: The system is designed for use by scholarship applicants, LGU staff, and scholarship coordinators of the Municipality of San Enrique. Its main purpose is to improve efficiency, organization, accessibility, and communication in handling scholarship records and applicant information. Specifically, it reduces reliance on manual record-keeping and supports faster dissemination of updates such as application results, interview schedules, and payout-related announcements.
71: However, the system is limited to records management, monitoring, and notification functions only. It does not replace government-mandated and office-based procedures such as the physical submission of required documents, actual document verification, accounting review, auditing, final approval by authorized personnel, and the actual release of scholarship funds. Uploaded documents in the system are intended only for digital submission, pre-screening, and reference, while official validation may still require physical copies and manual checking by the responsible offices.
72: The study is further limited by the accuracy and completeness of the data provided by users, the availability and stability of internet connectivity, and the reliability of third-party SMS services for OTP and notification delivery. In addition, the system does not provide real-time chat or direct messaging between applicants and LGU personnel, since communication is limited to system-generated and SMS-based notifications. Lastly, the system is developed specifically for the scholarship procedures of the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental, and is not intended for direct implementation in other LGUs without further modification and customization.
73: Definition of Terms
74: API (Application Programming Interface). A software intermediary or set of rules that allows two separate applications to communicate and exchange data or functionality. In this study, it refers specifically to the use of external services integrated into the system.
75: Bootstrap. A popular, free, and open-source front-end framework containing pre-written HTML, CSS, and JavaScript components and tools for creating responsive, mobile-first websites and web applications efficiently. The specific front-end framework utilized in the development of the Automated Scholarship Records Management System, as listed in the software requirements. It provides the foundational code for the design, layout, and responsiveness of the system's user interfaces (like the admin dashboard and applicant portal).
76: Dashboard. A homepage on a website giving access to different elements of the site's functionality. The primary graphical user interface (GUI) of the Automated Scholarship Records Management System intended for LGU staff use.
77: Database. An organized collection of structured information or data, typically stored electronically in a computer system. Within the study, this refers to the centralized digital storage system created to securely hold all relevant information about scholarship applicants and recipients, including personal details, academic background, application status, submitted documents, and disbursement details, ensuring organized record-keeping and easy access for authorized LGU staff.
78: Disbursement. The act of paying out money especially from a fund. In this study, it refers to the process and record of paying out scholarship funds to a qualified and approved scholar.
79: SMS Notification. A notification received on a mobile device indicating a new text message has arrived via the Short Message Service (SMS). A feature integrated into the system to automatically send text messages to applicants informing them about application confirmations, status updates, interview schedules, and disbursement announcements. It includes bulk SMS capabilities for wider notifications.

147: a. Results and Discussions
148: This section presents the evaluation results of the Automated Scholarship Records Management System based on feedback collected from end-users and IT experts. The assessment involved the use of two structured survey instruments based on the ISO 25010 quality model to assess user-specific experience. Both questionnaires utilized a 4-point Likert scale, where 4 indicated the highest level of agreement or satisfaction.
149: The evaluation results given by the IT experts show that the Automated Scholarship Records Management System performed very well in all eight ISO 25010 software quality characteristics. Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Compatibility, and Maintainability each obtained a mean of 3.8000, while Usability obtained 3.7333, Reliability obtained 3.6500, Security obtained 3.7600, and Portability obtained the highest mean of 3.8667. The overall mean for IT experts was 3.7763, verbally described as Strongly Agree and interpreted as Excellent. These results indicate that the system was able to meet the required technical and functional expectations of the evaluators. The findings suggest that the system is capable of supporting scholarship operations in a dependable, secure, and usable manner.
150: These findings are similar to the study of Casimiro et al. (2026), who found that information quality, system quality, and service quality positively influenced user satisfaction and net system benefits in a scholarship application information system. In the same way, Dizon et al. (2025) reported high evaluation results for their scholarship monitoring system with data analytics, showing that web-based scholarship systems can perform well when they are designed to improve efficiency, transparency, and information management. The strong ratings given by the IT experts in the present study show that the developed system possesses the software quality characteristics needed for actual implementation.
151: The evaluation results given by the end-users also show a very favorable assessment of the system. Functional Suitability obtained a mean of 3.8333, Performance Efficiency obtained 3.7500, and Usability obtained 3.7667. The overall mean for end-users was 3.7833, verbally described as Strongly Agree and interpreted as Excellent. These results indicate that the respondents found the system easy to use, useful in performing scholarship-related tasks, and effective in improving the management of records and applications. The high rating in Functional Suitability suggests that the users believed the system was able to perform the tasks expected from it, while the high rating in Performance Efficiency indicates that the system responded well and helped reduce delays in scholarship processing. The result for Usability further shows that the users were able to understand and operate the system with convenience.
152: This result is consistent with the study of Bagaporo and Espinosa (2025), whose online scholarship management system improved efficiency, information management, control, and service delivery through centralized digital processes. It is also similar to the findings of Ablen et al. (2025), who reported that a scholarship management and tracking system helped address delayed processing, inaccurate recordkeeping, and weak communication among scholarship stakeholders. In the same manner, Samillano et al. (2025) found that the LYDO Scholarship Management System improved the accuracy, efficiency, and organization of scholarship processing while reducing staff workload and helping applicants stay informed of important updates. The positive assessment of the end-users in the present study shows that the system was able to provide practical benefits to both scholarship applicants and LGU staff.
153: The total mean of the system evaluation was 3.7798, which was verbally described as Strongly Agree and interpreted as Excellent. This indicates that both IT experts and end-users had a consistently positive evaluation of the Automated Scholarship Records Management System. The closeness of the IT expert mean and end-user mean further suggests that the system was able to satisfy both technical standards and actual user needs. This result shows that the system is not only technically acceptable, but also useful and responsive in the context where it will be applied. Since the system obtained excellent evaluation ratings from both technical experts and actual users, it can be said that the developed system successfully addressed the need for a more organized, efficient, and user-friendly scholarship records management platform.
154: Overall, the results of the study show that the Automated Scholarship Records Management System is highly acceptable in terms of both software quality and user experience. The system was able to meet the functional and operational needs of scholarship applicants and LGU staff while also satisfying technical evaluation standards. The results further show that a centralized and automated scholarship system can improve records organization, tracking, communication, and administrative efficiency. These findings support the implementation of the system in the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental as a practical tool for improving scholarship operations.

162: e.1. Testing
163: The Automated Scholarship Records Management System was tested to determine whether it performs according to its intended functions and requirements. The testing process focused on verifying the accuracy, reliability, usability, and security of the system in handling scholarship-related transactions. Specifically, the researchers examined whether the system could properly manage applicant registration, login with OTP verification, scholarship application submission, document uploading, status monitoring, announcement posting, notification delivery, interview scheduling, and record management.
164: The researchers conducted testing in several stages. First, individual modules of the system were checked to confirm that each feature worked correctly on its own. These included user authentication, applicant profile management, application processing, document handling, and notification functions. Second, integration testing was performed to ensure that related modules worked properly when used together. For example, the researchers verified whether applicant registration correctly connected to profile creation, whether uploaded documents were linked to the correct application records, and whether status updates triggered the proper notifications. Lastly, overall system testing was carried out to assess the performance of the entire system in a simulated real-use environment.
165: In addition, usability testing was conducted to determine whether the system was easy to understand and operate for its intended users, particularly scholarship applicants, staff, and coordinators. The researchers observed how users navigated the system, completed tasks, and interpreted the interface. Minor issues such as confusing labels, navigation difficulties, or unclear instructions were noted for improvement. Security and access control were also checked to ensure that only authorized users could access restricted features and sensitive scholarship records.
166: Through this testing process, the researchers were able to identify errors, validate system functions, and confirm whether the proposed system was ready for implementation. The results of the testing served as the basis for refining the system and ensuring that it could provide dependable support for the scholarship operations of the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental.

172: f. Findings
173: The following are the key findings of the study based on the testing and evaluation of the Automated Scholarship Records Management System for the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental.
174: The system was found capable of performing its intended core functions. These include applicant registration, OTP-based verification, scholarship application submission, document uploading, application status monitoring, interview scheduling, announcement posting, notification delivery, and scholar record management. During system testing, these functions operated according to the intended workflow of the scholarship process and were able to support both applicant-side and administrator-side transactions.
175: In terms of record organization, the system was found to provide a more structured and centralized method of storing scholarship-related information. Applicant personal details, educational background, submitted requirements, application progress, and scholarship records were stored in a single database environment, making retrieval and monitoring more efficient compared to the previous manual process. This finding indicates that the system can help reduce the problem of scattered records and repetitive manual encoding.
176: The findings also showed that the application monitoring feature improved the tracking of applicants throughout the scholarship process. By using defined status levels such as Submitted, Initial Review, Pending Resubmission, For Interview, Eligible Awaiting SOA, Approved Pending Payroll, and Approved Finished, the system enabled staff to clearly determine the current standing of each applicant. This made monitoring more systematic and reduced confusion in identifying incomplete, pending, or completed applications.
177: Another important finding of the study was the effectiveness of the document management feature. The system was able to link uploaded files, such as grades, barangay residency, and statement of account, to the appropriate application records. It was also found that the document status and versioning functions supported better monitoring of pending, verified, rejected, and resubmitted requirements. This helped improve document tracking and reduced the difficulty of reviewing scholarship requirements using purely manual procedures.
178: In terms of communication, the system was found to improve the dissemination of scholarship-related updates. Through OTP verification, in-system notifications, announcement posting, and SMS-based alerts, applicants were provided with more direct access to important information such as application updates, interview schedules, and other notices. This finding suggests that the system can lessen reliance on delayed or inconsistent manual announcement practices.
179: The study further found that the role-based access of the system supported better security and control over scholarship records. The distinction between student, staff, and administrator accounts ensured that access to sensitive information and administrative functions was limited to authorized users only. The inclusion of audit logs, case notes, and notification records also strengthened accountability in system usage and transaction monitoring.
180: Overall, the findings of the study showed that the Automated Scholarship Records Management System is capable of improving the scholarship management process of the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental. The system addressed major issues related to record organization, application monitoring, document tracking, and communication. Although it does not replace manual procedures such as physical document verification, accounting review, and actual fund release, it was found to be a functional and reliable support system for scholarship records management.
181: Conclusions
182: Based on the findings of the study, it is concluded that the Automated Scholarship Records Management System can effectively support the scholarship operations of the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental in a more organized, efficient, and systematic way. The system successfully carried out its intended functions, particularly in applicant registration, OTP-based verification, scholarship application processing, document uploading and monitoring, interview scheduling, notification delivery, and scholarship record management.
183: The study also concludes that the system was able to address several challenges found in the existing manual process. By centralizing applicant records, application details, document submissions, and status tracking, the system improved the organization and accessibility of scholarship-related information. It also created a clearer and more manageable workflow for monitoring applicants through the various stages of scholarship processing.
184: In addition, the study concludes that the inclusion of features such as document status monitoring, version tracking, announcements, SMS notifications, role-based access, case notes, and audit logs strengthened the system’s functionality and reliability. These features helped improve communication, accountability, and control in managing scholarship transactions and records.
185: However, it is also concluded that the system mainly serves as a support tool for scholarship records management and does not replace manual and government-required procedures such as physical document verification, accounting review, final approval, and the actual release of scholarship funds. Despite these limitations, the developed system was found to be a practical and dependable solution that can greatly improve the scholarship management process of the LGU.
186: Overall, the researchers conclude that the Automated Scholarship Records Management System is an acceptable and effective solution for managing scholarship records and related processes in the LGU of San Enrique, Negros Occidental. Its implementation can help improve efficiency, strengthen record organization, and support more effective communication between the LGU and scholarship applicants.
187: Recommendations
188: Based on the study’s findings and conclusions, the following recommendations are presented.
189: The Local Government Unit (LGU) of San Enrique, Negros Occidental is encouraged to adopt the Automated Scholarship Records Management System as a support tool for handling scholarship applications, applicant information, document submissions, notifications, and other related transactions. Using the system may improve the efficiency, organization, and accessibility of scholarship operations compared to the current manual process.
190: It is also recommended that authorized LGU personnel, especially scholarship staff and coordinators, receive proper training before the system is fully implemented. Sufficient orientation and hands-on practice may help users better understand the system’s functions and reduce errors in data entry, status updates, and records management.
191: In addition, regular system maintenance and monitoring should be carried out after implementation. This should include periodic database backups, checking system performance, reviewing user access, and addressing any technical issues that may occur during actual use. Continuous monitoring may help maintain the system’s reliability, security, and long-term functionality.
192: The researchers further recommend the continued use of physical verification and government-required procedures alongside the digital system, particularly for official document validation, accounting review, auditing, final approval, and scholarship fund release. Since these processes are still beyond the scope of the developed application, proper coordination between manual and digital procedures remains important.
193: Lastly, future researchers are encouraged to enhance the system further by adding more advanced features such as online report generation, expanded analytics, automated qualification screening, email notifications, and wider interoperability for use in other local government units. Additional studies may also be conducted to improve the system’s scalability, usability, and overall functionality in similar institutional settings.
